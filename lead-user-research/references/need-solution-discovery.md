# Need–solution discovery (conceptual extension)

Source: Eric von Hippel & Georg von Krogh (2016), Identifying Viable “Need–Solution Pairs”: Problem Solving Without Problem Formulation, Organization Science 27(1), 207–221. https://doi.org/10.1287/orsc.2015.1023

The paper develops a conceptual alternative to problem-first search and proposes empirical investigation. The following operational rules are adaptations for this repository, not a demonstrated superior method. Frame the research decision before discovery, but do not require a fully formulated problem. Keep the existing eight phases, evidence standards and human authority.

| Operation | When useful | Record |
|---|---|---|
| Need-, solution-, or pair-led discovery | A suspected need, newly available capability, or observed valuable practice | Search direction, observed clue, candidate pairing, evidence vs hypothesis, next search |
| Positive deviance | Unusually favorable outcomes under comparable constraints | Baseline, outcome, comparable cases, resource/skill/context differences, alternative causes |
| Variable problem formulation | Current framing may conceal a more valuable intervention | Causal, purpose-level, substitute-activity or cross-domain alternatives; evidence and boundaries |
| Relative pairing viability | A pairing appears promising | Incremental benefit vs actual baseline, costs/effort, transfer constraints, externalities, evidence/unknowns |
| Innovation-origin tracing | A real adopted change is informative | Encounter/creation, need recognition, adoption and actual outcome; unknown chronology/motives remain unknown |
| Iterative reformulation | New observations challenge the accepted need/frame | Old/new frame, evidence, affected gates, approvals and revision decision |

## Execution

A: allow an open domain and decision, keep consequential scope unknown or human-approved. B: search from both landscapes; record unexpected practices and candidate applications. Do not require a stated problem in solution-led discovery, or treat an application inferred by an agent as an observed pair. Positive outcomes route investigation; test rival explanations and comparable constraints. C: collect atomic evidence and reconstruct innovation chronology as far as sources permit, without inventing causal or motivational narratives. Both LU1 and LU2 still require evidence on a relevant trend; a successful user or clever solution need not be a lead user. D: judge whether framing blind spots matter to the decision; repair proportionately. E: compare formulations and relative viability, separate need, principle and mechanism, preserve contradictions. F: freeze the human-accepted frame and criteria for each comparison. G: test whether both the proposed need and mechanism survive comparison with the baseline and alternatives. H: report the revised formulation and what remains unknown.

A material change within scope requires a change record and invalidation of dependent research decisions. Material scope/decision changes require explicit human approval. For post-freeze evidence use the existing PF## mechanism and resulting fingerprint; reassess sufficiency, interpretation and gates, invalidate affected approvals, criteria, comparisons, selection, decision and handoff; obtain new explicit acceptance before comparing again. Preserve superseded state in the change history or a separate study rather than rewriting the original freeze. Do not force this cycle without new evidence. If the repository's state controller cannot safely express a material reframe in the existing workspace, start a new approved study with provenance to the prior record.

Use the existing records as described in the state contract. No new required schema fields. Solution-led discovery and pair viability do not establish commercial demand, causal effect, or implementation authority. Market Opportunity Underwriting owns business-level economics. TRIZ and C-K theory are not mandatory extensions.
