# Lead User Research — Decision Brief

## Decision

Should the fictional product team run a three-week prototype validation of a structured AI handoff capability?

## Recommendation — TEST

In this fictional scenario, run a bounded three-week prototype validation focused on preserving decision state with less upkeep than manual ledgers.

## Why

- One qualified fictional Lead User episode demonstrates sustained high benefit from reducing handoff reconstruction.
- The independent abandonment case makes maintenance burden a decisive design and validation constraint.
- Derivative adoption cannot be counted as independent confirmation.

## Decisive evidence

- **[F2](#f2) — VERIFIED** — One fictional advanced operator sustained a structured handoff packet and reported substantially lower reconstruction effort while preserving decisions and constraints.
- **[F3](#f3) — VERIFIED** — The second fictional adoption is derivative and therefore does not add independent confirmation of either discovery or benefit.
- **[F4](#f4) — VERIFIED** — An independent fictional counterexample abandoned a detailed handoff ledger because its maintenance cost exceeded its recovery value.
- **[LU1](#lu1) — QUALIFIED** — Preserve current decisions, constraints, and open questions across AI-agent handoffs with less reconstruction effort. (An anonymized fictional multi-agent operator)

## Critical uncertainty

- Whether a lower-maintenance mechanism can preserve enough decision context to create net benefit
- Whether the need appears across independent real operators

## Action now

### A1 — Run a fictional three-week prototype validation of two handoff mechanisms

- **Owner:** Product validation lead
- **Timebox:** Three weeks
- **Deliverable:** A comparison of handoff recovery value, upkeep cost, and failure modes for M1 and M2
- **Success condition:** At least one mechanism reduces total handoff effort by 50% without increasing consequential decision loss relative to the current approach
- **Stop condition:** Stop if neither mechanism produces net time benefit after two consecutive observed handoffs or if consequential context loss increases
- **Decision at end:** Choose ACT, HOLD, or REJECT for a real discovery investment; do not select a production mechanism from this synthetic fixture

**Evidence to collect**

- Minutes spent preparing and recovering each handoff
- Decisions or constraints lost, repeated, or incorrectly revived
- Abandonment and workaround behavior
- Differences across at least three independent workflow lineages

## What would change this decision

- Move toward ACT only if independent real operators show net benefit after maintenance cost and no increase in consequential context loss.
- Move to REJECT if low-maintenance mechanisms repeatedly lose critical decisions or remain net-costly.

## What the evidence supports

- The fictional need is plausible enough to justify a bounded validation test.
- Maintenance burden belongs in the fitness conditions.

## What the evidence does not support

- Any claim about real users, prevalence, market size, willingness to pay, or production feasibility
- Selection of M1 or M2 as a product solution

## What could make us wrong

- The independent counterexample shows that a handoff artifact can cost more to maintain than it saves.
- The second adoption is derivative and supplies no independent benefit evidence.

## Opportunity shaping

### N1 — Preserve current decisions, constraints, and open questions across AI-workspace handoffs with materially less total effort than manual reconstruction or ledger upkeep.

**Passing fitness conditions**

- R1 — A handoff must preserve consequential decisions, constraints, and open questions without reinstating rejected choices.
- R2 — Preparing and recovering a handoff must require materially less total effort than reconstructing the state manually.

**Candidate mechanisms — not selected solutions**

#### M1 — Generate a bounded handoff packet from the current workspace that lists active decisions, constraints, rejected options, open questions, and evidence links for confirmation before transfer.

- Requirements: R1, R2
- Assumptions: The current workspace exposes enough structured state to generate an accurate packet; A short confirmation step costs less than manual reconstruction
- Risks: Summarization may omit or revive consequential decisions; Confirmation may become another maintenance burden
- Evidence needed next: Observed preparation and recovery time; Consequential omission and decision-revival rate; Abandonment or compensating behavior

#### M2 — Maintain a shared decision ledger that automatically shows changes since the receiving workspace last synchronized.

- Requirements: R1, R2
- Assumptions: Automatic change capture can keep manual upkeep below recovery value; Recipients can distinguish superseded from active decisions
- Risks: The ledger may accumulate noise or stale state; Synchronization errors may create false confidence
- Evidence needed next: Observed ledger maintenance time; Rate of stale or conflicting decisions; Recovery accuracy across independent workflows
