# Empirical enhancements to Lead User Research

These optional extensions preserve the v1.7 evidence and authority contract. They improve different operations; the integrated approach has not itself been validated as superior. Use only where the pending decision warrants them.

## Choose the operation

| Goal | Technique | Evidence and limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Find rare informants efficiently | Screen broad candidate pools, then pyramid adaptively; start from multiple independent branches | Stockstrom et al. (2016), 942 schoolchildren across 42 classes: referral search was more efficient than screening in this setting; not a commercial innovation trial. |
| Find informative starting points | Brokerage/betweenness in a relevant observed network | Kratzer et al. (2016), three studies: lead users tend to bridge groups; network position is not LU qualification. |
| Assess candidate lead-userness | Optional respondent-administered hierarchical scale | Paulus et al. (2025): 30 items across six facets; short form available. Table 2 (n=196) R² for innovation experience .683 vs .657 and intention .659 vs .569 (best prior scale per outcome). Not held-out prediction accuracy or an episode qualification cutoff. |
| Generate novel but applicable concepts | Analogous-domain knowledge, target-domain adaptation and recombination | Franke, Poetz & Schreier (2014): analogous-market ideas more novel but less immediately applicable. Separate novelty and applicability. |
| Improve experimental decisions | Theory-led comparisons, predictions and abandonment thresholds | Camuffo et al. (2024): four RCTs, 759 firms; evidence of more termination and selective pivoting, not validation of a combined Lead User workflow. |

Lilien et al. (2002) observed much higher forecast sales in a quasi-experimental 3M comparison; forecast sales are not actual revenue, and the projects were not randomized. The original method remains the foundation.

## Phase B: adaptive search

Define a trend, attribute, decision-critical uncertainty, and search boundary first. Use a public or consented relationship graph only if its edge meaning is relevant (e.g., referrals or collaboration, not mere co-mentions). Document collection window, missing populations and edges, directed/undirected treatment, metric and limits. Betweenness is a routing signal, not proof of expertise or lead-userness. No graph? Use ordinary screening/pyramiding. Avoid private or sensitive relationship inference. Do not equate independent branches with disconnected clusters if they share upstream evidence.

At each major search move, compare extending a chain, screening a new population, or investigating a candidate. Record alternatives, expected decision impact, effort/access, chosen move, actual effort and yield; avoid arbitrary scores and unobserved values. Stop or repair when the next proportionate move is unlikely to change the decision. Automated semantic search/clustering can prioritize candidates; sample classifications against independent human labels when material, report precision/recall only where reference labels permit, and record selection bias and error. Do not promote machine labels into evidence.

## Phase C: measurement

Paulus et al. retain ahead-of-trend (ideas, information, experience) and high expected benefit (problems, needs, gains). Use the published full or short instrument, item wording and scoring guidance where authorized, with actual respondents and a defined need/trend/time referent. Document version, response source, administration, missingness and limitations. Do not reconstruct answers or scores from traces, invent a scoring cutoff, impute missing answers without a justified published procedure, or present qualitative facet mapping as scale administration. The study also reports higher stated willingness to pay for concepts from high versus low scorers; hypothetical valuations do not establish purchases, or superiority of this scale over other instruments in selecting commercially successful concepts. A questionnaire is a diagnostic; observed relative advancement and concrete benefit evidence remain necessary for LU1/LU2.

## E–G: transfer, recombination and test

Extract the source principle, source constraints, target constraints and adaptation; retain contradictions, record observed implementation/repeated use/abandonment/diffusion with lineage, and distinguish applicability and novelty. Fit Check tests design fit, not performance; no concept quota or automatic selection. Seek actual collaborative lead-user development where appropriate. Test the causal explanation with typical target users under relevant conditions: comparator, prediction, observable result, threshold, stop and revision rule. Use actual behavior and payment when those are the question; hypothetical stated intent or model estimates are not equivalent. Route business-level economics to Market Opportunity Underwriting and counterfactual simulation to Behavioral Simulation under their evidence contracts.

## Proposed comparative evaluation — not executed

Preregister a multi-domain, matched-budget comparison: classical Lead User Research; current v1.7; and v1.7 with the optional enhancements. Randomize research tasks or teams where feasible, balance domain expertise and time, keep source access comparable, prevent leakage, and blind independent evaluators to condition. Predefine primary outcomes (cost/time per independently qualified episode and independently rated decision usefulness); report candidate precision/recall only with a defensible reference population, traceability, contradictory evidence discovery, concept novelty and applicability separately, and longitudinal propagation, adoption/payment and costly false positives where observable. Record attrition, uncertainty intervals, clustering by domain/team, negative outcomes and costs; plan sample size from target effect and variance before enrollment. An assurance fixture cannot establish empirical effectiveness. Test component contributions via ablation if feasible.
